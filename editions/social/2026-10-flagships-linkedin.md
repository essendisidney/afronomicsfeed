## 1. Africa's borrowing bill

Zambia's government paid 14.50% to borrow for a year in October 2025. On 1 October 2026 it paid 10.00%.

That 4.5-point fall is the largest in the nine African bill markets we track. But the map has split three ways:

• Done falling, for now: Kenya (9.04%), South Africa (7.54%) and Tanzania (6.28%) sit within 0.3 points of their policy rates.
• Market ahead of the central bank: Nigeria's one-year bill cleared at 15.85%, 10.65 points under a 26.50% policy rate. Ghana's has fallen eight auctions in a row.
• Dear and holding: Egypt at 25.67%, unchanged on a year.

Our base case, two alternatives, and the dates that will decide between them: Ghana on 18 November, South Africa on 19 November, Mozambique on 25 November.

## 2. The new map of who lends to African governments

At the auction dated 12 October, investors bid KES 50.2bn for Kenya's 91-day bill. The government asked for KES 8bn. The one-year bill fell short of its offer, again.

Lenders at home have money, but want to lend short. Bid cover at bill auctions, April to October:

• Kenya 1.50 (1.38 a year ago)
• Ghana 1.46 (1.03)
• Zambia 1.51 (1.08)

Lenders abroad want more. Kenya's 2034 dollar bond yield rose from 7.96% to 9.31% between 14 August and 8 October. The extra Kenya pays to borrow in shillings rather than dollars for the same maturity shrank from 4.78 to 3.50 points in four weeks.

## 3. KES 100,000 over the next year

KES 100,000 in the average Kenyan savings account earns KES 3,009 a year after tax.

In the one-year Treasury bill from the auction dated 12 October: about KES 7,680, fixed on the day you buy.

What happens to everything that floats depends on rates. We ran three paths to October 2027, each anchored to a level Kenya's one-year bill has actually reached:

• If rates fall back to April's 8.27%, the locked bill earns KES 558 more than rolling three-month bills.
• If they stay at 9.04%, KES 233 more.
• If they rise to April 2025's 10.39%, rolling wins by KES 342.

What to watch: the one-year bill above 9.5% or below 8.5% at any weekly auction, and the CBK's next MPC date.
