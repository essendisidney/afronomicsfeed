Lending to your government through a **Treasury bill** or **savings bond** is one of the safest ways to earn interest in your own currency (see [what a Treasury bill is](/explainers/learn-treasury-bills)). Here is what each government’s **own official source** says about how an individual can do it. Only the official channels below are listed; if anyone else asks you for money “to buy government bonds”, check first.

## Kenya: Treasury bills and bonds

*From the Central Bank of Kenya’s “Kenya securities” page:*

- **Minimum:** “If you’d like to purchase a Treasury bill, you must invest a minimum of Kshs. 50,000.” The same minimum applies to Treasury bonds.
- **Who:** “Individuals can invest directly through the Central Bank.”
- **How:** you need a bank account and a **CDS account** with the Central Bank, opened through its **DhowCSD** portal, which also has a mobile app.
- **Tax:** 15% withholding tax on Treasury bill returns for residents.
- **Rates:** set at weekly auctions: [every Kenyan result since 2011](/markets/kenya-tbills).

## Nigeria: FGN Savings Bond

*From the Debt Management Office’s offer for October 2026:*

| October 2026 offer | Terms |
| --- | --- |
| 2-year bond, due 14 October 2028 | **13.071%** a year |
| 3-year bond, due 14 October 2029 | **14.071%** a year |
| Offer open | 5 to 9 October 2026; settlement 14 October 2026 |
| Units | ₦1,000 each; **minimum ₦5,000**, then multiples of ₦1,000, up to ₦50,000,000 |
| Interest | paid **every quarter**: 14 January, April, July and October |

The offer states that the bond is listed on the Nigerian Exchange and qualifies as a government security within the meaning of the Company Income Tax Act and the Personal Income Tax Act “for Tax Exemption for Pension Funds, amongst other investors”. *EXAMPLE:* ₦5,000 in the 2-year bond at 13.071% pays about ₦163.39 each quarter (₦653.55 a year).

A new offer is published **every month** on the [DMO’s Savings Bond page](https://www.dmo.gov.ng/fgn-bonds/savings-bond), with the rates and how to apply; read the current one before you buy.

## South Africa: RSA Retail Savings Bonds

*From the National Treasury’s RSA Retail Savings Bonds site:* “There are three easy steps to follow to invest in a Retail Bond”:

1. **Register** your details with the National Treasury: online at [rsaretailbonds.gov.za](https://www.rsaretailbonds.gov.za/), or at the National Treasury, 240 Madiba Street. You receive a unique reference number.
2. **Apply:** select a bond and maturity.
3. **Pay** by internet banking or direct deposit at a bank, using your reference number.

Applicants under 18 need their parents’ or guardians’ written authority. Helpline: 012 315 5888, Monday to Friday, 08h00 to 16h00. Current rates are on the site’s **Rates** page.

## Tanzania: Treasury bills

*From the Bank of Tanzania’s Treasury bills investment calculator:* bills are sold at a **weighted average price** below face value, and the calculator applies **10% withholding tax** on the gain.

*From the calculator on 5 October 2026:* the 364-day bill’s weighted average price was **94.1017 per 100**. For example, a TZS 100,000 bill cost about TZS 94,101.70 and returned TZS 5,898.30, or about **TZS 5,308.50 after the 10% tax**: roughly 6.27% a year before tax.

The Bank of Tanzania publishes auction calls, results and an issuance calendar on its [Treasury bills page](https://www.bot.go.tz/TBills).

## Other countries

We have not yet found official guidance for individuals that we can quote for **Ghana, Uganda, Egypt, Zambia, Malawi or Mozambique**. Each sells bills at weekly auctions (see the [T-bill monitor](/markets/tbills)); ask your central bank or a licensed bank how individuals take part, and add the guide here when it is confirmed.
