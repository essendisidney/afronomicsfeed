## 1. Three trends that moved this week

### Trend 1. Debt workouts moved to the front of the queue at the Bangkok meetings, with Senegal as the test case
- **What happened.** On 15 Oct the US Treasury's undersecretary for international affairs, Erin Browne, after
  co-chairing the Global Sovereign Debt Roundtable with the IMF and World Bank heads, called Senegal's restructuring
  request a "great example" of progress under the G20 Common Framework and said Washington wants the work done "in
  less than a year" (Africanews, 15 Oct, READ run 381). The IMF's $2.2bn, 36-month staff-level agreement with Senegal
  from early September still needs Board approval; the IMF had put Senegal's 2023 deficit at 12.3% of GDP against the
  4.9% the previous government reported, and public-sector debt at 132% of GDP at end-2024 (Africanews, 2 Sep, READ
  run 379).
- **What our own data shows.** Senegal's regional auctions have thinned. Bids divided by the amount sought were 1.57
  and 1.63 on 14 and 28 Aug; 1.09, 1.05 and 1.06 on 11 Sep, 25 Sep and 9 Oct. On 9 Oct the Treasury sought XOF 80bn and
  accepted XOF 74.4bn; its one-year bill cleared at 7.87% (REPO, `data/waemu/auctions.json`, UMOA-Titres notices).
  Burkina Faso's one-year bill cleared at 3.73% on 7 Oct, with bids 1.83 times the amount sought (same file).
- **Why it matters.** Readers in Dakar and the diaspora want to know whether a restructuring touches their savings.
  Buyers (frontier-debt funds, D-004) hold or price WAEMU paper and need the timeline next to the auction tape; we are
  the only English source that lays the two side by side. Kenya is on the same calendar: no funded programme, and an
  IMF Article IV mission "expected in Nairobi early December 2026" (National Treasury statement of 12 Oct, via The
  Kenya Times, 13 Oct, READ run 378).
- **Our view.** The Senegal timeline becomes the most-watched African debt story into 2027. Whether the regional
  (UMOA-Titres) market is in or out of the perimeter is the question our readers and buyers will ask; we have **not**
  found a source that answers it, so we do not say.
- Sources: [Africanews, 15 Oct 2026](https://www.africanews.com/2026/10/15/us-wants-to-see-senegals-debt-restructuring-completed-within-a-year/);
  [Africanews, 2 Sep 2026](https://www.africanews.com/2026/09/01/senegal-and-imf-agree-new-22-billion-loan-programme/);
  [The Kenya Times, 13 Oct 2026](https://thekenyatimes.com/economy/imf-to-send-team-to-kenya-months-to-elections/);
  [Capital FM](https://capitalfm.africa/kenya-us-review-imf-engagement-ahead-of-december-mission/) (undated on the page; same Treasury statement).

### Trend 2. The IMF says inflation is turning up just as some central banks are cutting
- **What happened.** The IMF's October Regional Economic Outlook update, briefed in Bangkok on 15 Oct, puts
  sub-Saharan growth at 4.5% in 2026 and 2027 and median inflation rising from 3.4% (2025) to 4.3% (2026) and 5% (2027).
  The African Department director said interest takes about 12% of government revenue in a typical country, that heavier
  reliance on domestic banks can crowd out credit, and that "monetary tightening might be necessary in many countries"
  (NAN, 15 Oct, READ run 377). Semafor (14 Oct) reports the 4.5% as up from July's 4.3% and quotes the IMF that it "masks
  substantial divergence", with oil importers most exposed (READ run 377).
- **Against that, the rate moves.** The CBN cut its MPR by 3.5 points to 23.00% on 21–22 Sep; the Bank of Ghana held at
  14% with August inflation at 5.0% (Nairametrics, 26 Sep, READ run 379). Kenya's CBR is 8.75% (REPO,
  `data/policy_rates.json`, dated 07/10/2026).
- **What our data shows.** Ghana's 91-day bill cleared at 4.62% on 16 Oct (REPO, `data/ghana/tbill_auctions.json`),
  below the 5.0% August inflation that Nairametrics reports: a negative real yield on that pairing (Analysis; check the
  GSS release before publishing). Kenya's 91-day cleared at 8.75% on 19 Oct (REPO) against 6.76% inflation in September,
  which we compute from KNBS index levels in `data/kenya/cpi.json` (156.47 vs 146.56). Nigeria's 91-day stop rate was
  15.50% on 14 Oct (REPO), 7.5 points under the reported MPR.
- **Why it matters.** Real yields are the reader question ("is my T-bill beating prices?") and a fund question (carry).
  The Real-Yield Board (horizon scan 3C) is a cheap reader tracker the gate allows; this week supplied its headline.
- **Our view.** If the IMF's inflation path holds, the next round of MPCs (Malawi 29 Oct, Ghana 18 Nov, SARB 19 Nov)
  is more likely to hold than cut. Signpost: any MPC statement that cites fuel or fertiliser prices.
- Sources: [NAN, 15 Oct 2026](https://nannews.ng/imf-projects-4-5-growth-for-sub-saharan-africa-in-2026-2027/);
  [Semafor, 14 Oct 2026](https://www.semafor.com/article/10/14/2026/imf-raises-growth-outlook-for-sub-saharan-africa);
  [Nairametrics, 26 Sep 2026](https://nairametrics.com/2026/09/26/african-mpr-nigeria-ranks-3rd-as-cbn-cuts-rate-in-september-2026/).

### Trend 3. In Kenya, money is crowding into the shortest bill while the reserve cushion thins
- **What happened.** At the auction valued 19 Oct, the CBK offered KES 8bn of 91-day bills, received KES 49.8bn of bids
  and accepted KES 34.2bn at 8.75%; the 364-day bill drew KES 6.6bn of bids for KES 10bn offered (66%) at 9.04%
  (REPO, `data/kenya/tbill_auctions.json`). The week before (valued 12 Oct) the 91-day drew KES 50.2bn for KES 8bn
  (same file). The CBK's bulletin of 16 Oct puts the 15 Oct auction at KES 68.5bn of bids for KES 28.0bn (244.6%), and
  usable reserves at USD 14,689m (5.9 months of import cover) on 15 Oct, down from USD 15,088m on 17 Sep (READ run 380).
  Our own brief this week reported a third week of NSE losses with net foreign selling of USD 9.5m (Standard Investment
  Bank wrap, 19 Oct, cited in `content/briefs/nse-weeks-of-losses-foreign-selling.md`), and Nedbank's NCBA offer went
  unconditional on 19 Oct (`content/briefs/nedbank-ncba-takeover-what-changes-and-when.md`).
- **Why it matters.** Savers are choosing liquidity over locking in a year, which is exactly what Call it asks players to
  guess. For buyers, short-end crowding and falling reserves ahead of an IMF mission are the Kenya risk story; the
  bond tape (which funds buy) sits behind it.
- **Our view.** The short-bill preference persists until the December IMF mission gives a read on the financing gap.
  What would make us wrong: a one-year bill fully subscribed at or below 9.0% before the next CBK MPC (date not
  published in `data/mpc_calendar.json`).
- Sources: [CBK Weekly Bulletin, 16 Oct 2026](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/uploads/weekly_bulletin/1082633218_Weekly%20CBK%20Bulletin%20October%2016%202026.pdf);
  [SIB weekly wrap, 19 Oct 2026](https://sib.co.ke/reports/kenya-weekly-market-wrap-19-october-2026/) (as cited in our brief; not re-read this week).
