# Africa demand check: what Africa wants and can pay for (14 Oct 2026)

The founder asked us to make sure we sell what Africa wants and can pay for. This note sets out what our own
numbers say today, what they cannot yet tell us, and how we find out by the end of October.

## What our numbers say (last 30 days, `page_views`, visitors with a country only)

| What they looked at | From Africa | From outside Africa |
|---|---:|---:|
| API calls (`/api/...`) | 3 | 203 |
| Institutional pages (frontier debt, licensing, pricing, data API) | 9 | 46 |
| Market data pages (`/markets/...`) | 56 | 37 |
| Reader money tools (Kenya rates, rate check, loans, savers story, learn) | 47 | 62 |
| Everything else (home, countries, news, stories) | 226 | 129 |

- **Countries.** Kenya 286 views. Then Nigeria 28, South Africa 16, and Egypt, Tanzania, Ethiopia, Uganda,
  Senegal and Zambia with 1 to 3 each. A large share of US, Irish and Dutch views are probably cloud servers and
  scripts, not people.
- **Leads, survey answers and payments.** None from anyone (one Paystack test payment).

**Reading it.**

1. The paid institutional product (the API and frontier debt pages) is used almost only from outside Africa.
2. African visitors, mostly Kenyan, come for market pages and personal-money tools: Kenya rates, the rate checker,
   loans and the savers story.
3. These are small numbers. They show where attention goes, not what anyone will pay for. Nobody in Africa or
   elsewhere has paid yet.

## What Africa can pay (already built, D-014/D-015)

| Who | Price | How they pay |
|---|---|---|
| Kenyan readers (Pro) | KES 1,500 a month | M-Pesa or card |
| Local institutions | about USD 100–150 a month (Analyst) or USD 600–900 (Team and API), in local currency | Kenya: M-Pesa or card at checkout; elsewhere: card link or invoice |
| Global funds | list price | card or invoice |

## How we find out by 31 October

1. **Ask institutions directly (in flight).** Wave 4 went to nine African institutions on 14 Oct at local prices,
   with a follow-up on 22 Oct. Their replies, or silence, are the most direct evidence.
2. **Ask readers.** Put the one-question survey ("What would you pay for?") on the Kenyan reader tools
   (`/rates/kenya`, `/rates/kenya/check`, the savers story). Use options priced in shillings, e.g. "T-bill result
   alert by SMS/WhatsApp, KES 50 a month". Today the survey only runs on institutional pages, which Africans barely
   visit.
3. **Five conversations (founder, about 15 minutes each).** One each with a bank treasury dealer, a fund manager's
   fixed income analyst, a SACCO manager, a chama treasurer and a pension trustee. Three questions: what do you check
   each week, where do you get it, and what would you pay to stop doing that by hand?
4. **No new dataset or product until the evidence is in** (master plan v2: datasets are evidence-gated).

## Decision rule (end of October)

| What we see | What we do |
|---|---|
| 2 or more African institutions take a pilot at the local price | Lead with local institutions: wave 5 to SACCOs, banks and pension schemes; Kenya first |
| African readers answer the survey and pick a shilling-priced option | Build that one thing (e.g. paid auction alerts via M-Pesa) before anything else |
| Only global funds respond | Sell the data abroad, which pays for the African coverage; keep the reader side free with partner income |
| Nobody responds | Change the offer, not the audience: talk to the five people above before writing more |
