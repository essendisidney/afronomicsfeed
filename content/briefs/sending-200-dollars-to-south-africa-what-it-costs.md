A teacher in London sends $200 home to her parents in Durban every month. Part of it never arrives: it goes on the fee and the exchange rate. How much depends almost entirely on which service she uses.

The World Bank priced **25** services sending $200 from the United Kingdom to South Africa in its survey of January to March 2025. The cheapest, Remitly, cost **$4.18**, or 2.09% of the money. The average service cost **$14.08**, or 7.04%.

## The same $200, two sending countries

| Sending from | Services surveyed | Cheapest service | Average service |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| United Kingdom | 25 | $4.18 (Remitly) | $14.08 |
| United States | 18 | $3.84 (WorldRemit) | $13.68 |

The cost is the World Bank's measure: the fee plus the margin hidden in the exchange rate, as a share of $200.

## What it means

**For a family sending monthly from the UK** (Analysis): the cheapest service against the average saves **$9.90** on each $200. Over a year of monthly transfers that is **$118.80**: money that reaches home instead of staying with the provider. From the United States the gap is $9.84 a transfer, or $118.08 a year.

**For the spread of prices** (Facts): from the United Kingdom the dearest service surveyed cost $33.46 on $200, against the average of $14.08. From the United States the dearest cost $22.42, against the average of $13.68.

**For the person receiving** (Analysis): the exchange rate matters as much as the fee. A service that advertises "no fee" can still cost more if it gives fewer rand per dollar.

**What to check before you send** (Facts): the total cost on the provider's own quote screen, meaning the amount that arrives in rand, not just the fee. Then compare it with the next cheapest.
