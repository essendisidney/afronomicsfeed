## Post text

Senegal paid 7.87% for one-year money on 9 October. Burkina Faso paid 3.73% two days earlier. Same CFA franc, same central bank.

A year ago the two were 0.43 points apart, and Senegal was the cheaper borrower.

One-year bill yields at UMOA-Titres auctions, a year ago and now:
- Senegal: 7.18% to 7.87% (+0.69 points)
- Mali: 7.63% to 4.90% (−2.73)
- Côte d'Ivoire: 6.88% to 4.06% (−2.82)
- Burkina Faso: 7.61% to 3.73% (−3.88)

The gap between Senegal and Côte d'Ivoire went from 0.30 to 3.81 points.

Read the depth too. Senegal accepted 87% of the money bid, from 22 bidders. Burkina Faso accepted 20%, from five. The lowest yields were set on the smallest sales.

What to watch: Senegal's next one-year sale, and whether Côte d'Ivoire and Burkina Faso keep taking a small share of bids.

Every figure is from UMOA-Titres' own result pages; the table and arithmetic are in the brief.
