Every time someone in Nairobi sends $200 home to Kampala, part of it is lost to the fee and the exchange rate. How much depends almost entirely on which service they use.

The World Bank priced **27** services sending $200 from Kenya to Uganda in its survey of January to March 2025. The cheapest, Dahabshiil, cost **$5.56**, or 2.78% of the money. The average service cost **$23.80**, or 11.90%.

## The same $200, three sending countries

| Sending from | Services surveyed | Cheapest service | Average service |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kenya | 27 | $5.56 (Dahabshiil) | $23.80 |
| United Kingdom | 33 | $3.04 (Sendwave) | $10.08 |
| Tanzania | 10 | $15.00 (MoneyGram) | $127.24 |

The cost is the World Bank's measure: the fee plus the margin hidden in the exchange rate, as a share of $200.

## What it means

**For a family sending monthly from Kenya** (Analysis): the cheapest service against the average saves **$18.24** on each $200. Over a year of monthly transfers that is **$218.88**: money that reaches home instead of staying with the provider. From the United Kingdom the gap is $7.04 a transfer, or $84.48 a year.

**For the spread of prices** (Facts): from Kenya the dearest service surveyed cost $70.70 on $200, against the average of $23.80. From Tanzania, where only 10 services were surveyed, the average was $127.24, or 63.62% of the money, against $15.00 for the cheapest.

**For the person receiving** (Analysis): the exchange rate matters as much as the fee. A service that advertises "no fee" can still cost more if it gives fewer Uganda shillings per dollar.

**What to check before you send** (Facts): the total cost on the provider's own quote screen, meaning the amount that arrives in shillings, not just the fee. Then compare it with the next cheapest.
