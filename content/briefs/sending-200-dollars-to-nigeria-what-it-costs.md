Every time someone in Johannesburg sends $200 home to Nigeria, part of it is lost to the fee and the exchange rate. How much depends almost entirely on which service they use.

The World Bank priced **20** services sending $200 from South Africa to Nigeria in its survey of January to March 2025. The cheapest, Mama Money, cost **$8.02**, or 4.01% of the money. The average service cost **$26.26**, or 13.13%.

## The same $200, six sending countries

| Sending from | Cheapest service | Average service |
| --- | --- | --- |
| South Africa | $8.02 (Mama Money) | $26.26 |
| Italy | $0.02 (Remitly) | $11.36 |
| United Kingdom | $2.40 (Sendwave) | $8.76 |
| Germany | $0.42 (WorldRemit) | $8.02 |
| United States | $1.54 (MoneyGram) | $7.24 |
| Canada | $1.88 (WorldRemit) | $6.58 |

Between 13 and 24 services were surveyed from each country. The cost is the World Bank's measure: the fee plus the margin hidden in the exchange rate, as a share of $200.

## What it means

**For a family sending monthly** (Analysis): from South Africa, the cheapest service against the average saves **$18.24** on each $200. Over a year of monthly transfers that is **$218.88**: money that reaches home instead of staying with the provider. From Italy the gap is $11.34 a transfer, or $136.08 a year.

**For the spread of prices** (Facts): from South Africa the dearest service surveyed cost $69.28 on $200, against the average of $26.26.

**For the person receiving** (Analysis): the exchange rate matters as much as the fee. A service that advertises "no fee" can still cost more if it gives fewer naira per dollar.

**What to check before you send** (Facts): the total cost on the provider's own quote screen, meaning the amount that arrives in naira, not just the fee. Then compare it with the next cheapest.
