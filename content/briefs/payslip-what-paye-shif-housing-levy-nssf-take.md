Two Kenyans each earn KES 50,000 a month on paper. On payday, KES 39,029 reaches the bank. The other **KES 10,971** has gone to four places before the money ever moves.

That is 21.9% of the salary. At KES 100,000 it is **29.6%**. Here is where it goes, and why the share climbs so fast.

## The four deductions

**PAYE** (Facts): income tax, charged by the Kenya Revenue Authority in bands of monthly taxable pay: 10% on the first KES 24,000, 25% on the next KES 8,333, then 30% up to KES 500,000. A personal relief of KES 2,400 a month comes off the tax.

**SHIF** (Facts): the Social Health Insurance Fund premium, 2.75% of gross pay, at least KES 300 a month.

**Affordable Housing Levy** (Facts): 1.5% of gross pay. The employer pays another 1.5% on top.

**NSSF** (Facts): since February 2026, 6% of pay up to KES 108,000 a month, so at most KES 6,480 from the employee; the employer matches it.

SHIF, the Housing Levy and NSSF come off **before** tax is worked out, as KRA's PAYE page sets out, so each one also lowers the PAYE a little.

## What it means

**At KES 20,000, no PAYE** (Analysis): after the other three deductions, taxable pay is KES 17,950. The 10% band gives KES 1,795 of tax, less than the KES 2,400 relief, so nothing is due. The deductions that remain are NSSF, SHIF and the levy.

**The raise you actually get** (Analysis): between KES 50,000 and KES 100,000, of every extra KES 1,000 on the payslip, **KES 628** reaches take-home. Above KES 108,000, NSSF stops rising and the figure is KES 670. At KES 20,000 it is KES 898.

**What your employer also pays** (Facts): on KES 50,000, a matching KES 750 Housing Levy and KES 3,000 to NSSF. Neither shows in take-home, but both are part of what the job costs.

**What to check on your payslip** (Analysis): that SHIF is 2.75% of gross, the levy 1.5%, and that PAYE is worked out after those and NSSF come off. Put your own figures into the [net salary calculator](/rates/kenya/net-salary) to see each line.

## What to watch

NSSF's limits have moved each February as the NSSF Act is phased in; the Year 4 limits took effect in February 2026, so watch for NSSF's next notice to employers early in 2027. PAYE bands and the SHIF and levy rates change only through Parliament or new regulations: if any moves, we will update this table.
