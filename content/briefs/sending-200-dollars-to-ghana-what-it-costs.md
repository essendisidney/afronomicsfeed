A nurse in Hamburg sends $200 home to her mother in Kumasi every month. Part of it never arrives: it goes on the fee and the exchange rate. How much depends almost entirely on which service she uses.

The World Bank priced **33** services sending $200 from Germany to Ghana in its survey of January to March 2025. The cheapest, Remitly, cost **$3.56**, or 1.78% of the money. The average service cost **$19.76**, or 9.88%.

## The same $200, five sending countries

| Sending from | Services surveyed | Cheapest service | Average service |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Germany | 33 | $3.56 (Remitly) | $19.76 |
| United Kingdom | 38 | $1.42 (Unity Link) | $13.72 |
| United States | 28 | $0.64 (MoneyGram) | $10.00 |
| Canada | 22 | $3.46 (WorldRemit) | $13.28 |
| Netherlands | 26 | $1.04 (Unity Monetary Services BV) | $9.60 |

The cost is the World Bank's measure: the fee plus the margin hidden in the exchange rate, as a share of $200.

## What it means

**For a family sending monthly from Germany** (Analysis): the cheapest service against the average saves **$16.20** on each $200. Over a year of monthly transfers that is **$194.40**: money that reaches home instead of staying with the provider. From the United Kingdom the gap is $12.30 a transfer, or $147.60 a year; from the United States, $9.36, or $112.32 a year.

**For the spread of prices** (Facts): from the United Kingdom the dearest service surveyed cost $83.44 on $200, against the average of $13.72. From Germany the dearest cost $59.14, against the average of $19.76.

**For the person receiving** (Analysis): the exchange rate matters as much as the fee. A service that advertises "no fee" can still cost more if it gives fewer cedis per dollar.

**What to check before you send** (Facts): the total cost on the provider's own quote screen, meaning the amount that arrives in cedis, not just the fee. Then compare it with the next cheapest.
