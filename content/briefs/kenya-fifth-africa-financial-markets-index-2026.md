Kenya's capital market now ranks fifth in Africa. A year ago it ranked seventh.

The 2026 Africa Financial Markets Index, compiled by Absa Group and the Official Monetary and Financial Institutions Forum (OMFIF), gives Kenya a score of 65 out of 100, Business Daily Africa reported on 14 October 2026. For a saver holding shares or bonds on the Nairobi Securities Exchange (NSE), the index is a yardstick of how easy the market is to use: how much trades, how much is disclosed, how hard it is to get money in and out.

## Who is ahead

**Facts.** Four countries rank above Kenya: South Africa, Mauritius, Namibia and Uganda.

| Rank | Country | Score, 2026 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | South Africa | 85 |
| 2 | Mauritius | 77 |
| 3 | Namibia | 71 |
| 4 | Uganda | 68 |
| 5 | Kenya | 65 |

*Source: Absa Africa Financial Markets Index 2026, as reported by Business Daily Africa (14 October 2026) and TechTrends Kenya (19 October 2026).*

**Facts.** The index covers 29 countries this year. The reported average score is 56, and 21 of the 29 countries scored higher than in the previous edition.

## What moved Kenya

**Facts.** Business Daily credits the NSE's first dollar-denominated listings and a strong run in Kenyan shares and bonds. Kenya scored 100 on market transparency, level with South Africa, according to the reporting.

**Facts.** The same reports name market depth and the development of local pension funds as Kenya's weaker scores.

## What it means

**For investors on the NSE** (Analysis): the gains came from activity, meaning more trading and new products. Those can move quickly in either direction. A higher rank this year reflects a busier market over the period the index measured, not a permanent change.

**For the gap with Uganda and Namibia** (Analysis): three points separate Kenya from Uganda. The pillars where Kenya trails are the ones that take years to change: how much long-term money local pension funds hold, and how much of it sits in the market.

**For the regulator and the exchange** (Analysis): transparency is already at the maximum score. Further gains for Kenya would have to come from the other pillars, which is where the Capital Markets Authority and the NSE's product plans meet the index.

## What to watch

- Kenya's full pillar scores in the Absa/OMFIF index report, and whether depth and pensions improve in the 2027 edition.
- Further dollar-denominated listings on the NSE, and how much they trade.
- The next edition of the index, and whether Kenya holds fifth place.
