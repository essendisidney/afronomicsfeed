## 1. Three trends that moved this week

### Trend 1. South Africa's mini-budget borrows more to buy back its own bonds
- **What happened.** Finance Minister Enoch Godongwana tabled the Medium-Term Budget Policy Statement on 21 Oct.
  The Treasury raised this year's borrowing requirement by about R54bn against February, "mainly to finance a
  domestic bond buyback program designed to smooth out redemptions" (Moneyweb, SEARCH). **The new total is not
  settled:** one extract gives R434.2bn and another R443.2bn for 2026-27, against R380bn in February. Read the
  Treasury table before using either. Gross debt is reported to stabilise at 78.4% of GDP in 2025-26, below the
  78.9% in February, with a primary surplus of 1.7% of GDP this year (SEARCH, reports of 21 Oct). The Mail &
  Guardian headline reports R10bn of temporary fuel relief (SEARCH, 21 Oct). News24 headlined "Bonds rally, rand
  gains" the same day (SEARCH, headline only).
- **What our own data shows.** South Africa's 91-day bill cleared at 7.12% and the 364-day at 7.50% on 19 Oct
  (REPO, `data/southafrica/tbill_auctions.json`). The SARB repo rate is 7.25% (REPO, `data/policy_rates.json`). The
  91-day bill pays less than the policy rate (Analysis).
- **Why it matters.** Readers: fuel relief and a buyback are budget lines a saver can feel. Buyers: a buyback
  changes the supply of South African government bonds, which sit in GBI-EM Edge. **We do not hold South African
  bond auctions** (a known Edge gap, `master-plan-v2.md` §3). We can report the plan; we cannot show the tape.
- **Our view.** The next test is the rating calendar. A search extract says Moody's and S&P review South Africa on
  27 Nov and Fitch on 11 Dec (BusinessTech, SEARCH, undated on the extract). **We could not confirm these dates**
  on the agencies' own calendars. Signpost: the SARB MPC on 19 Nov (REPO, `data/mpc_calendar.json`).
- Sources: [Moneyweb, "SA raises borrowing requirement to fund bond buyback"](https://www.moneyweb.co.za/in-depth/budget/sa-raises-borrowing-requirement-to-fund-bond-buyback/) (21 Oct, SEARCH);
  [News24, "Bonds rally, rand gains…", 21 Oct 2026](https://www.news24.com/business/economy/bonds-rally-rand-gains-as-godongwana-outlines-ratings-friendly-debt-trajectory-20261021-0910) (headline);
  [Mail & Guardian, 21 Oct 2026](https://mg.co.za/news/south-africa/2026-10-21-budget-2026-godongwana-announces-r10bn-in-temporary-fuel-relief/) (headline);
  [EWN, 21 Oct 2026](https://www.ewn.co.za/2026/10/21/mtbps-2026-godongwana-tables-mid-term-budget-with-new-fiscal-anchor-laws-on-the-horizon) (SEARCH; 78.4%, 1.7%);
  [BusinessTech](https://businesstech.co.za/news/?p=880466) (SEARCH; rating dates, unconfirmed).

### Trend 2. Kenya's rush into three-month bills stopped, with KES 1.7tn falling due within a year
- **What changed since last week.** Last week's note reported money crowding into the 91-day bill. That ended. At
  the auction valued 26 Oct, the CBK offered KES 8bn of 91-day bills and received KES 14.3bn of bids (179%),
  against KES 49.8bn (622%) the week before. It accepted KES 14.3bn at 8.75%. The 364-day bill again drew less than
  offered: KES 6.8bn of bids for KES 10bn (68%) at 9.03%. Across the three bills, bids were KES 28.8bn for KES 28bn
  offered (103%), against KES 68.5bn (245%) on 19 Oct (REPO, `data/kenya/tbill_auctions.json`).
- **The news.** People Daily (22 Oct) reports that Kenya has KES 1.7tn of public debt due within a year and a
  refinancing test before June 2027. It ties this to the IMF's October World Economic Outlook, which, as reported,
  did not forecast Kenya's costs specifically. It also reports KES 66.8bn of domestic bond switches and buybacks in
  the year to June 2026 (SEARCH).
- **Why it matters.** Savers: the one-year bill still pays more than the three-month (9.03% against 8.75%), and
  fewer people are taking it. Buyers: a Treasury that must roll KES 1.7tn will lean on the bond tape that funds buy.
- **Our view.** One week is not a trend change. The bill results do not say who bid or why. What would tell us more:
  the next two weekly bill auctions and the IMF Article IV mission expected in early December.
- Sources: [People Daily, 22 Oct 2026](https://peopledaily.digital/business/imf-warning-raises-stakes-for-kenyas-ksh1-7t-debt-bill) (SEARCH);
  CBK results notice of 26 Oct, linked in `data/kenya/tbill_auctions.json`.

### Trend 3. Senegal pays more on its own market while the IMF Board date stays unpublished
- **What changed since last week.** Last week the story was thinner demand. This week the price moved. On 23 Oct
  Senegal sought XOF 50bn, drew XOF 69.4bn of bids and accepted XOF 55.0bn. Its one-year bill yielded 8.06%, up
  from 7.87% on 9 Oct. Its five-year bond yielded 8.24%, up from 7.82%. On the three-year bond it took only
  XOF 0.5bn of the XOF 9.0bn bid. Three days earlier, on 20 Oct, Côte d'Ivoire's one-year bill yielded 3.89% (REPO,
  `data/waemu/auctions.json`, UMOA-Titres notices). Same currency, same central bank: Senegal paid 4.17 points more

- **Our view.** Senegal's regional yields keep rising until a Board date is published. What would make us wrong: a
  one-year bill below 7.87% before the Board meets.
- Sources: [UMOA-Titres, Senegal issue of 23 Oct 2026](https://www.umoatitres.org/fr/emission/emission-simultanee-de-bons-et-dobligations-du-tresor-du-senegal-du-23-10-2026/);
