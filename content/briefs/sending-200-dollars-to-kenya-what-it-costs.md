Every time someone in London sends $200 home to Kenya, part of it is lost to the fee and the exchange rate. How much depends almost entirely on which service they use.

The World Bank priced **32** services sending $200 from the United Kingdom to Kenya in its survey of January to March 2025. The cheapest, Skrill, cost **$3.14**, or 1.57% of the money. The average service cost **$8.88**, or 4.44%.

## The same $200, four sending countries

| Sending from | Cheapest service | Average service |
| --- | --- | --- |
| United States | $0.54 (Walmart2World) | $10.40 |
| United Kingdom | $3.14 (Skrill) | $8.88 |
| Canada | $4.34 (Royal Bank of Canada) | $14.56 |
| South Africa | $8.76 (Mama Money) | $20.90 |

Between 27 and 36 services were surveyed from each country. The cost is the World Bank's measure: the fee plus the margin hidden in the exchange rate, as a share of $200.

## What it means

**For a family sending monthly** (Analysis): from the United States, the cheapest service against the average saves **$9.86** on each $200. Over a year of monthly transfers that is **$118.32**: money that reaches home instead of staying with the provider. From South Africa the gap is $12.14 a transfer, or $145.68 a year.

**For the person receiving** (Analysis): the exchange rate matters as much as the fee. A service that advertises "no fee" can still cost more if it gives fewer shillings per dollar.

**What to check before you send** (Facts): the total cost on the provider's own quote screen, meaning the amount that arrives in shillings, not just the fee. Then compare it with the next cheapest.
