# Afronomics launch kit (20 Oct 2026)

Ready-to-paste copy for every launch site and directory worth Afronomics' time, in the order to do them. Each needs
the founder's own account; none can be submitted by a routine. Check each site's form on the day: field limits and
rules change.

**Rules that apply everywhere**
- Never ask friends, groups or "upvote rings" to vote: Product Hunt and Hacker News penalise or remove launches that do.
  Ask people to *look* and *comment*, never to upvote.
- Every number in the copy below is on the live site today. If you change a number, check the page first.
- Link with UTM tags so the desk shows which site sent readers, e.g.
  `https://www.afronomicsfeed.com/?utm_source=producthunt&utm_medium=launch&utm_campaign=launch-2026`.
- Reply to every comment on launch day, within the hour if you can. That is most of the result.

Treasury bill rates in ten African markets, read from each central bank the day it publishes. A weekly Sovereign Bill

**Long description:**
Afronomics tracks the price of money in Africa. We read Treasury bill and bond auction results from central banks in
ten markets on the day they publish, and turn them into plain answers: what a government pays to borrow for a year,
what KES 100,000 earns in a bill against a money market fund, what Fuliza really costs.

- **Rates & data:** T-bill monitor across ten markets, the African Sovereign Bill Index (weekly, since 2016), WAEMU bond
  auctions, policy rates, Eurobond yields, currencies.
- **Tools:** M-Pesa and Fuliza cost calculator, debt payoff planner, net salary (PAYE, SHIF, Housing Levy, NSSF),
  Treasury bill calculator, IPO calculator.
- **Receipts:** long investigations that show the documents: who lends to African governments, the Housing Levy money
  trail, Fuliza's true cost.
- **Call it:** a weekly game where readers guess Kenya's T-bill results before the CBK publishes them.
- **For institutions:** the same data as an API and files, with a 30-day pilot.

Every number links to the publisher's own document. Free to read. Information, not advice.

## 1. Product Hunt (biggest single day; plan it)

- **When:** a Tuesday, Wednesday or Thursday. A launch day runs from 00:01 Pacific (10:01 Nairobi). Schedule it in
  advance on the "Submit" form so it goes live at 00:01.
- **Needs:** maker account (yours), name, tagline (60), description (260), topics, 3–5 gallery images, link, a
  "first comment" from the maker.
- **Maker's first comment:**

> Hi Product Hunt 👋 I'm Sidney, founder of Afronomics.
>
> African governments borrow at auctions every week, and the results are public, but they sit in central-bank PDFs that
> almost nobody reads. Meanwhile people in Nairobi pay Fuliza fees that work out at over 200% a year without knowing it.
>
> Afronomics reads those results from the central banks in ten markets the day they publish and turns them into plain
> answers: what a government pays to borrow, what your savings earn, what a loan really costs. Every figure links to its
> source document.
>
> Free to read. Try the Fuliza calculator, or play this week's "Call it" and guess Kenya's T-bill rates before the CBK
> publishes them. I'd love your feedback on what to track next.

## 2. Hacker News, "Show HN"

- **When:** a weekday morning US time (about 15:00–17:00 Nairobi). One post only; do not repost the same day.
- **Title (80 characters max):** `Show HN: Afronomics – African T-bill auctions, read from 10 central banks` (73)
- **URL:** https://www.afronomicsfeed.com/markets/tbills
- **First comment (post it yourself straight after):**

> I built this because the price of money in Africa is public but unreadable: auction results are PDFs on ten different
> central-bank sites, in different formats, some in French. Scrapers read each one the day it's
> published (GitHub Actions on a schedule; Python readers per publisher; pdfplumber for the PDFs), write JSON into the
> repo, and a Next.js site renders it. Every figure links back to the source document, and there's a public log of
> when each result was first seen.
>
> Hard parts: publishers that change formats without notice, buybacks printed on the same page as new issues, and
> results with no release time at all. Happy to go into any of it.

HN readers like the method: answer technical questions in detail, never argue.
