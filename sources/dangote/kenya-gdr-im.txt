          This Information Memorandum (Approved as a Short Form Prospectus) should be read and construed in

          Capital Markets Authority (“CMA”) and Nairobi Securities Exchange (“NSE”). Prospective investors should
          carefully consider the matters set forth in this document. Investors should read the Information Memorandum

          conditions.

          Markets Act as read together with Regulation 29 of the POLD Regulations.
                                                                                  
          This document is an Information Memorandum inviting applications for Global Depository Receipts (“GDR”)
          proposed to be issued in Kenya, up to the maximum amount as shall be allocated by DPRP/the Underlying Issuer
          to the GDR Issuer. As at the date of this Information Memorandum, the DPRP IM does not prescribe an Allotment
          Policy and the same is expressed to be at the discretion of the board of directors of DPRP. The GDR Issuer has
          not secured a guaranteed allocation and the number of shares to be allocated to it is not guaranteed or
          ascertainable as at the date of this Information Memorandum. The issuance of the GDRs will be subject to
          successful allocation by the Issuer and the maximum number of GDRs covered by this Information Memorandum
          shall not represent more than 100% of the issued shares of DPRP allocated to the Issuer pursuant to the
          Allotment Methodology adopted by it.

          RISKS RELATING TO CURRENCY FLUCTUATIONS AND REPATRIATION OF CAPITAL     
                                                                                  
          Depreciatory Movements in the Issuer's Home Currency Relative to the Kenyan Shilling (KES)
                                                                                  
          The Underlying Shares backing the GDRs are denominated, traded, and valued in Naira (₦). However, the GDRs
          will be listed and traded on the NSE in Kenyan Shillings (KES). Consequently, any depreciation or devaluation of
          the Naira (₦) against the KES will automatically reduce the KES-equivalent value of the GDRs, irrespective of the
          operating performance or share price of the Company in its home market. 
                                                                                  
          Given that the Issuer’s reporting currency is the US Dollars, it intends to declare dividends in US Dollars, subject
          to applicable laws, efficiency of remittance, regulatory requirements, financing arrangements and Board of
          Directors approval/recommendation.                                      
                                                                                  
          Divided distributions where payable in USD and foreign exchange differences in the conversion of USD to KES
          may impact the final amounts received by the GDR Securities.

          Holders of GDRs do not possess direct shareholder status or legal title to the Underlying Shares, which are

          corporate governance permissions, execute proxy voting, and enforce shareholder rights. If a corporate dispute
          arises, individual investors cannot sue the Company directly as a shareholder of record; the Issuer as bare trustee
          does not also have any obligation to initiate cross-border legal actions.
                                                                                  
          RISKS RELATING TO CORPORATE GOVERNANCE AND ASYMMETRIC INFORMATION FLOW  
                                                                                  
          Structural Information Gaps and Delayed Proxy Voting Mechanics          
                                                                                  
          Due to the cross-border logistical chain connecting the Nigeria Exchange (“NGX”) and the NSE, there may be
          material time lags in the transmission of corporate notices, financial reports, and proxy materials to investors in
          Kenya. By the time the Issuer processes, translates (if applicable), and transmits voting instructions to the
          Custodian, the hard cut-off dates specified by the Company’s Articles may have passed. As a result, Kenyan GDR
          holders face the risk of being unable to vote their shares or participate in critical corporate actions within the
          required timeframes.                                                    
                                                                                  
          RISKS RELATING TO CROSS-BORDER TAXATION AND DOUBLE TAXATION FRICTION    
                                                                                  
          Deductions from Foreign Source Withholding Taxes                        
          Cash dividends distributed by the Company on the Underlying Shares are subject to the domestic tax laws of
          Nigeria. Under current legislation, a statutory withholding tax of 10% is applied at the source prior to the
          remittance of funds to the Issuer. This foreign deduction will directly diminish the net dividend yield received by
          Kenyan GDR holders, and the Company is under no obligation to "gross up" or compensate investors for these
          foreign tax deductions.                                                 
                                                                                  
          Double Taxation Liabilities and Lack of Treaty Relief                   
                                                                                  
          There is currently no active Double Taxation Agreement (DTA) ratified between the Republic of Kenya and the
          Republic of Nigeria. Consequently, investors in Kenya risk facing legal double taxation—where distributed
          returns are taxed first at the source country and subsequently subjected to income or withholding tax under the
          Income Tax Act (Cap. 470) of Kenya, materially depressing net investment yields.

          Symmetric Trading Suspensions Across Exchanges                          
          The market price and trading liquidity of the GDRs on the NSE are structurally tied to the active trading of the
          Underlying Shares on the NGX. If trading in the Underlying Shares is halted, suspended, or disrupted on the
          home exchange due to high volatility, regulatory investigations, or systemic market failures, the CMA and the
          NSE will likely initiate a parallel trading freeze on the inward GDRs. During such periods of suspension, Kenyan
          investors will be unable to trade, liquefy, or value their GDR positions.
                                                                                  
          Failure to Maintain Free-Float Capital Requirements                     
                                                                                  
          In accordance with the CMA Policy Guidance Notes on Inward Listings, the Company is required to maintain a
          minimum public free-float of 15% of the total issued GDR pool among investors in Kenya. If institutional buyers
          corner the market or if the local investor base thins out significantly over time, the GDRs may fall below this 15%
          mandatory free-float threshold. A failure to rectify this imbalance could result in an illiquid market, wide bid-ask
          spreads, regulatory fines from the CMA, or the eventual compulsory delisting of the GDRs from the NSE.

          SECTION 3: Mechanics of the Dangote Kenya GDR Programme                 
                                                                                  
          An unsponsored inward Global Depository Receipt (GDR) is a financial instrument that allows local investors to
          trade shares of a foreign company on their own domestic stock exchange, without the formal participation or
          consent of the foreign company itself.                                  
                                                                                  
          While the Dangote Kenya GDRs are unsponsored, the Issuer has received an in-principle approval from the
          Company of its support to the GDR issuance and will apply for the Underlying Shares in the ordinary course of
          the Company's initial public offering, in the same manner as any other applicant. As such. at the time investors
          apply for the GDRs, the Underlying Shares will not yet have been allocated or deposited with the Issuer and will
          only be issued to the GDR Issuer following the Allotment Date, subject to the Allocation Methodology adopted
          by the Company.                                                         
                                                                                  
          HOW THE DANGOTE KENYAN GDR WILL WORK                                    
          Step 1: Investors will apply, as per the application process on pg. 22 of this IM, by filling in the application form
               and paying for the application IN FULL.                            
                                                                                  
          Step 2: The GDR will submit an aggregate application for the total number of Underlying Shares equivalent to
               the applications received for the GDRs adjusted in accordance with the GDR ratio.
          Step 3: On allocation of the Underlying Shares to the GDR Issuer, the GDR Issuer will allocate and issue the
               Receipts in line with the GDR Allotment Methodology to each investor in Kenya.
                                                                                  
          Step 4: The Registrar will then credit the GDRs to the investor’s CDSC account (as per their application).
          Step 5: On the Listing Date, the GDR Shares will list on the Main Investment Market Segment (“MIMS”) of the
               Nairobi Securities and be tradeable as any other shares listed on the exchange.
                                                                                  
          The GDR works as a pass-through vehicle, and all corporate actions on the Underlying Shares shall be made to
          the GDRs.                                                               
          Should the demand for the GDRs increase post the IPO, the GDR is able to acquire more Underlying Shares from
          the secondary market at the NGX to meet this demand.

                                      SCHEDULE 2                                  
                                                                                  
                             PROVISIONS FOR MEETINGS OF HOLDERS                   
          1.   Convening: The Depositary may at any time, and shall on the written request of Holders holding not
               less than ten per cent. of the GDRs then outstanding, convene a meeting of Holders.
                                                                                  
          2.   Notice: At least twenty-one days' notice (exclusive of the day on which the notice is given and the day
               of the meeting) of every meeting shall be given to Holders in accordance with Condition 19, specifying
               the date, time and place of the meeting, the manner in which Holders may attend and vote (including
               by electronic means) and the terms of any resolution to be proposed.
          3.   Chair: The meeting shall be chaired by a person nominated by the Depositary or, failing such
               nomination, elected by the Holders present.                        
                                                                                  
          4.   Attendance: A Holder may attend and vote in person, by a duly authorised representative or by proxy.
               The Depositary may permit attendance and voting by electronic means, and a Holder so attending shall
               be treated as present.                                             
                                                                                  
          5.   Quorum: The quorum for a meeting to consider an Extraordinary Resolution shall be two or more
               persons present holding or representing not less than fifty per cent of the GDRs then outstanding and,
               at any adjourned meeting, two or more persons present holding or representing not less than twenty-
               five per cent. of the GDRs then outstanding. The quorum for any other meeting shall be two or more
               persons present holding or representing not less than ten per cent. of the GDRs then outstanding. If a
               quorum is not present within thirty minutes of the time appointed, the meeting shall be adjourned for
               not less than fourteen nor more than forty-two days, and notice of the adjourned meeting shall be
               given in accordance with paragraph 2 save that seven days' notice shall suffice.
          6.   Voting: Every resolution shall be decided on a poll, on which each Holder present shall have one vote
               for each GDR held or represented.                                  
                                                                                  
          7.   Extraordinary Resolution: “Extraordinary Resolution” means a resolution passed at a meeting of
               Holders duly convened and held in accordance with this Schedule by a majority of not less than seventy-
               five per cent. of the votes cast, or a resolution in writing signed by or on behalf of Holders holding not
               less than seventy-five per cent. of the GDRs then outstanding, or consent given by electronic means
