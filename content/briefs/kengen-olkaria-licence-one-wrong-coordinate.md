The licence was meant to describe a rectangle of 204 square kilometres at Olkaria. One of its four corner points was recorded as 21,000 instead of 192,000. On paper, the rectangle became a triangle.

That is the finding of Kenya's Auditor-General, as reported by Business Daily Africa on 14 October 2026. Some of the wells and steam pipes that KenGen built now sit outside the area its licence legally covers. KenGen is listed on the Nairobi Securities Exchange, so the question of who holds the rights to that ground is a question for its shareholders too.

## What the auditor found

**Facts.** According to the reporting, KenGen applied for the geothermal licence in February 2000. The ministry issued it on 19 September 2008. The licence fixes the field by four corner coordinates, and one of them was recorded as 21,000 rather than 192,000.

**Facts.** The energy ministry acknowledged the error in 2017. In 2018 part of the original area was allocated to another geothermal developer. As of the audit in August 2026, the licence had not been corrected, despite repeated requests from KenGen, Business Daily reports. The auditor warned that KenGen risks losing rights over geothermal investments that fall outside the boundary.

## What it means

**For shareholders** (Analysis): a well is only as secure as the licence over the ground beneath it. Steam fields take years and large sums to drill. Where the paper boundary and the physical wells disagree, the company's claim on part of that investment rests on a correction that has been pending for nine years.

**For the grid** (Analysis): the audit, as reported, describes a risk to rights, not a stoppage. Nothing in the reporting says any well has stopped producing. How much of KenGen's output depends on the wells outside the boundary is the number that would size the risk, and it has not been published.

**For the overlap** (Analysis): the 2018 reallocation matters because it happened after the ministry knew the boundary was wrong. Whether the area given to the other developer overlaps KenGen's working wells is not stated in the reporting.

## What to watch

- Whether the Ministry of Energy and Petroleum issues a corrected licence, and on what date.
- Whether KenGen addresses the finding in a disclosure on the Nairobi Securities Exchange or in its annual report.
- Whether Parliament's Public Investments Committee takes up the audit query.
